## 1. Decision Title

**The web estate is rationalised from 6 properties to at most 3 B2C
storefronts plus 1 B2B trade portal, all on SaaS/PaaS platforms, with product
data from a single source and availability exclusively from the inventory/order
hub. Self-managed Magento infrastructure is retired. The single-vs-split
platform question is resolved by evaluation against the B2B parity-plus test.**

**ADR Status**: Proposed (rationalisation and hub-sourcing accepted in
principle; platform selection pending evaluation and the joint checkpoint with
Project 004)

## 3. Context

Six properties, six catalogues, one small digital team. The Magento portal is
capable but self-managed — a patching and skills liability (SD-2). The five
Shopify storefronts serve brand-principal expectations but multiply content
work and MAP-pricing risk (SD-1). Web availability drifts from hub truth
(SD-3), quietly reintroducing the oversell problem Project 001 exists to kill.

## 4. Decision

1. **Storefront rationalisation:** ≤ 3 B2C storefronts (brand-cluster model:
   e.g. components/electronics, wheels/performance, house-brand retail) +
   1 B2B portal. Final count and clustering: CEO decision after brand
   consultation (T-1).
2. **SaaS/PaaS only:** no self-managed commerce infrastructure survives (G-2).
3. **Hub-sourced availability:** storefronts may not hold or compute their own
   stock figures (BR-004, FR-007); orders orchestrate through the hub.
4. **CRM identity:** accounts authenticate against the Project 002 customer
   master (BR-006).
5. **Platform selection:** Shopify Plus (single) vs Shopify+BigCommerce
   (split) vs BigCommerce (single), decided by evaluation in which the **B2B
   parity-plus scripted demo (BR-005) is a gate, not a score** — any platform
   failing it is eliminated regardless of other merits.

## 5. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — Keep 6 properties, integrate each to the hub | Least brand disruption | Rejected: content/pricing ops scale linearly with properties; fails G-1 |
| B — Consolidate to a single mega-store | Maximum simplification | Rejected: destroys brand-principal presentation expectations (STK-G6); MAP/brand risk |
| **C — Rationalised multi-storefront, SaaS, hub-sourced (chosen)** | Pattern above | **Accepted** |
| D — Replatform B2B only; leave 5 Shopify stores | Halves the project | Rejected: leaves SD-1/SD-3/SD-4 unsolved on the highest-volume channel |

## 6. Rationale

1. **Ops leverage:** content edited once and syndicated is the only model a
   small digital team sustains across brands (O-1).
2. **Inventory truth end-to-end:** the hub-first rule at the web tier is what
   makes G-3 (zero web oversell) and Project 004's C&C promise (G-7)
   simultaneously achievable.
3. **Risk transfer:** retiring self-managed Magento converts a security
   obligation into a vendor SLA (O-2).
4. **Decision hygiene:** making B2B parity-plus a *gate* prevents the
   single-platform convenience argument from silently degrading the wholesale
   channel that produces 60% of revenue.

## 7. Consequences

- **Positive:** sub-linear content ops; one identity and consent surface;
  ERP cutover invariant preserved (no web→MYOB coupling); Shopify-skills reuse
  likely.
- **Negative / accepted:** brand principals lose dedicated storefronts —
  managed via per-brand experiences within clustered stores; SEO migration
  effort is real (DAT-003); if the split option wins, two platforms are
  operated permanently.
- **Conditions:** joint checkpoint with Project 004 before platform sign-off;
  brand consultation before the CEO count decision; parity-plus demo evidence
  attached to the evaluation record.
