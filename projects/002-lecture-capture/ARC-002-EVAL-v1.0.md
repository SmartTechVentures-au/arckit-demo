## Document Purpose

This framework is the control that resolves the project's central dispute. Two senior stakeholders hold publicly stated, opposed positions on the platform outcome [S-C1], and ARC-002-RISK rates the resulting risk (R-001) as the highest-likelihood risk in the register. The agreed treatment is not to adjudicate between the positions but to **fix the criteria before the products are compared** — converting an executive disagreement into a decidable question.

BR-004 therefore requires this document to be **signed by Cassandra Rhodes, Dr. Benny Moog and Grace Tanaka before any vendor engagement**, and applied without amendment through to recommendation. An unsigned framework does not satisfy BR-004, and criteria amended after issue void the control entirely.

**This document does not recommend a platform.** It defines how one will be chosen.

## 1. Evaluation Overview

### 1.1 Purpose

To select one primary platform for the Learning Capture capability category (BR-001), through a process whose outcome is defensible to an unsuccessful supplier, to the Education Committee, and to the party whose stated position is not selected.

### 1.2 Evaluation Principles

1. **Criteria before products.** Weightings are fixed and signed before any supplier is approached (BR-004). Nothing in Sections 3 to 5 may change after issue.
2. **Weights are derived, not negotiated.** Category weightings are computed from the priority ratings in ARC-002-REQ — a prioritisation the Education Committee already approved [RR-C1]. The derivation is shown in full at Section 4.1 so it can be audited rather than argued.
3. **Gates are not tradeable.** Security, accessibility and data portability are pass/fail. They sit outside the scoring space so they cannot be offset by strength elsewhere.
4. **Evidence tier caps the score.** A claim evidenced only by vendor marketing cannot score as highly as one demonstrated hands-on. Section 5.3 makes this mechanical rather than a matter of evaluator judgement.
5. **Positions are declared, not excluded.** Panel members with stated preferences score, but declare their interest and score independently before moderation.
6. **Doing nothing is an option on the ballot.** "Retain both platforms, bounded" is evaluated as Option D, not treated as failure. The RIFF process explicitly permits closing a request [SGP-C1].

### 1.3 Deviation from the Standard ArcKit Template — Recorded Deliberately

The default ArcKit evaluation template allocates **45 of 100 points** to Project Approach and Methodology (20) and Team Qualifications and Experience (25), with a further 15 to Relevant Experience. That distribution is correct for a **systems integrator or professional services procurement**, where the thing being bought is a delivery team.

This is a **product procurement**. The university is buying a SaaS platform, not a project team. Sprint methodology, key personnel CVs and team composition are largely irrelevant to whether the platform captures a timetabled lecture and publishes it within four hours.

Applying the default weighting would allocate almost half the available score to factors that do not differentiate the candidates, while compressing capability, integration and exit — the three areas where ARC-002-RSCH found the candidates genuinely differ — into a single 35-point "Technical Approach" bucket.

**The categories have therefore been restructured around the requirement set.** Supplier capability and service quality are retained at 8 points (Section 4.9), which is where "company experience" legitimately belongs in a product purchase. This deviation is recorded here rather than applied silently, and the panel may reject it at the weighting workshop.

Failure of any mandatory gate eliminates the option regardless of its score. Gates are assessed **before** scoring begins, and results are recorded with evidence.

### 3.1 Mandatory Gate Checklist

| ID | Requirement | Source | Evidence required | Owner |
|----|-------------|--------|-------------------|-------|
| **MQ-1** | University SSO with MFA enforced; **no local accounts** and no platform-native passwords, including for room devices and service identities | NFR-SEC-001, REQ-031 | Hands-on test against the university IdP in a trial tenant. Vendor attestation alone is insufficient | Tobias Ohm |
| **MQ-2** | WCAG 2.2 Level AA conformance for the platform **as it will be configured** — see qualification below | NFR-C-002, REQ-029, Principle 14 | Current VPAT or independent audit report, **plus** hands-on assessment of the student player and the recording list | Dr. Benny Moog with Jazmin Field |
| **MQ-3** | Bulk export of recordings **with captions and metadata** in open, documented formats, at any time and on termination, without additional fee | NFR-I-002, REQ-034, Principle 9 | Practical export executed on a sample during evaluation, plus the contractual term in writing | Eleanor Frame with Sam Okafor |
| **MQ-4** | Australian data residency for recordings, transcripts and derived analytics — **or** a written statement of storage *and processing* locations sufficient to support an APP 8 assessment | NFR-C-001, DR-006, REQ-030 | Written vendor statement covering storage and processing separately | Eleanor Frame |
| **MQ-5** | Submission complete, on time, and in the required format, with the cost envelope submitted separately | Process | Procurement check | Grace Tanaka |

### 3.2 Gate Design Notes — Read Before Applying

Three deliberate design decisions, each recorded because a naive application of these gates would produce an unfair or absurd result.

**MQ-2 admits a dated remediation plan.** ARC-002-RSCH found that one candidate has announced WCAG 2.2 AA with an independent audit while another publishes a 2.1 AA position with no public VPAT. Gating strictly on a *published* 2.2 AA claim would eliminate a candidate on the currency of its documentation rather than the accessibility of its product. **MQ-2 therefore passes if the option either (a) evidences 2.2 AA conformance, or (b) provides a dated, contractually-committed remediation plan reaching 2.2 AA before cutover, with 2.1 AA evidenced now.** A candidate that can evidence neither fails.

**MQ-3 has an escape valve, and it escalates rather than voids.** The same research found no candidate demonstrating full open-format export of media *with* captions and metadata on published evidence — this is a market-wide gap, not a candidate weakness. If **all** options fail MQ-3 on practical test, the procurement does not thereby fail: the gate escalates to the Operations Committee with the market position documented, and the Committee decides whether to accept a reduced export capability as a contract term or to select Option D. **What the panel must not do is quietly relax the gate to let candidates through.** Principle 9 exists because a platform that cannot be left cannot be rationalised.

**MQ-1 admits no exceptions.** Two tools in the wider estate already breach REQ-031 by permitting local accounts [PC-C1]. This procurement will not add a third. There is no remediation-plan route on this gate.

### 3.3 What Is Deliberately *Not* a Gate

| Considered | Decision | Reasoning |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| LTI 1.3 at section-level granularity | **Scored (C.2), not gated** | Research found the incumbent's own guidance steers Blackboard migrations to LTI 1.1 and that LTI 1.3 does not support section-level linking. Gating this would eliminate the incumbent on a documented limitation that may be resolvable, and the limitation may be shared across the market. It is the sharpest documented integration difference found, so it earns heavy scoring weight instead |
| Native timetable-driven auto-capture | **Scored (A.1), not gated** | Research found one candidate requires per-meeting user action or a premium licence template to automate recording. This is material and is scored heavily, but gating it would eliminate the Principle 19 option before its cost case has been heard |
| Caption accuracy threshold | **Scored (B.2), not gated** | Accuracy is a continuum, and the test set (dependency D-8) does not yet exist. A numeric gate set before the test set is built would be arbitrary |
| Financial stability / company size | **Scored (I.2) at low weight** | All shortlisted suppliers are established in the sector; a stability gate would be theatre |

## 4. Evaluation Criteria (100 Points)

### 4.1 Weight Derivation — Auditable, Not Negotiated

ARC-002-RISK identifies weight-setting as the point where R-001 most likely materialises: "each party recognises that whoever sets the weights sets the outcome". The agreed control is to anchor weights to the register's own prioritisation rather than negotiate them fresh under adversarial conditions.

**Method.** Every requirement in ARC-002-REQ within evaluation scope was assigned to a category and scored by its stated priority. The two priority vocabularies were normalised: MoSCoW (BR/FR/INT/DR) and criticality (NFR).

| Priority as written | Normalised tier | Points |
|---------------------|-----------------|--------|
| MUST_HAVE / CRITICAL | Tier 1 | 3 |
| SHOULD_HAVE / HIGH | Tier 2 | 2 |
| COULD_HAVE / MEDIUM | Tier 3 | 1 |

**Result.** 58 in-scope requirements, 158 weighted points, distributed as follows. The requirement-derived categories are scaled to 77 points, leaving 15 for Commercial and 8 for Supplier — the two dimensions no requirement expresses.

| Category | Reqs | Weighted pts | Raw share | **Final weight** |
|----------|------|--------------|-----------|------------------|
| **A. Capture & Delivery Capability** | 12 | 33 | 20.9% | **16** |
| **B. Accessibility & Captioning** | 4 | 10 | 6.3% | **5** |
| **C. Integration & Provisioning** | 10 | 29 | 18.4% | **14** |
| **D. Privacy, Security & Compliance** | 12 | 33 | 20.9% | **17** |
| **E. Portability, Migration & Exit** | 8 | 24 | 15.2% | **12** |
| **F. Operability & Support** | 10 | 26 | 16.5% | **13** |
| *Requirement-derived subtotal* | *56* | *155* | *100%* | *77* |
| **H. Commercial & Cost** | — | — | — | **15** |
| **I. Supplier & Service** | — | — | — | **8** |
| **TOTAL** | | | | **100** |

**Category G (Discipline Exception) is excluded from the core 100** and scored separately at Section 4.10. Conflict C-6 in ARC-002-REQ requires the exception to be decided concurrently but not to distort the core comparison — a 2-point category inside the main score would do exactly that.

**Two honest observations about this derivation:**

Accessibility receives only 5 points, which will look low relative to its institutional importance. That is what the method produced: the category contains four requirements. The floor is protected by gate MQ-2 regardless, and the 5 points score *quality above the floor* — principally caption accuracy, which ARC-002-RSCH identifies as the sharpest student-impact differentiator. **The panel may argue this up at the workshop.** If it does, the derivation must be re-run and re-signed, not adjusted by hand.

Privacy, Security & Compliance carries the largest single weight (17) because it contains 12 requirements of which 8 are Tier 1. This is a consequence of the register, not a thumb on the scale — but it is worth the panel noticing, because it means an option that is strong on capability and weak on security cannot win.

### 4.2 Scoring Scale

Every sub-criterion is scored 0–4. The sub-criterion's weight is then applied.

| Score | Rating | Definition |
|-------|--------|------------|
| **4** | Strong | Fully meets the requirement, demonstrated. No material gap. Would require no workaround or contract remedy |
| **3** | Adequate | Meets the requirement with a minor gap, workaround or configuration dependency that the university can absorb |
| **2** | Partial | Meets the requirement in part. A material gap requiring a build, a contract remedy, or an accepted reduction in scope |
| **1** | Weak | Barely addresses the requirement. Significant build or workaround required, with delivery risk |
| **0** | Absent | Does not meet the requirement, or no evidence provided |

**No half-marks.** If evaluators cannot choose between two adjacent scores, the lower applies and the disagreement is recorded for moderation.

## 5. Evidence Rules

### 5.1 Evidence Hierarchy

| Tier | Evidence type | Maximum score permitted |
|------|--------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Demonstrated hands-on by the university, on university systems or a trial tenant | 4 |
| 2 | Contractually committed in writing | 4 |
| 3 | Vendor technical documentation, dated and specific | 3 |

## 6. Scoring, Thresholds and Decision Rules

### 6.1 Score Aggregation

1. Each scorer submits scores independently, with a one-line justification per sub-criterion and an evidence tier reference.
2. Scores are recorded by the secretariat **before** moderation.
3. Divergence of 2 or more points on any sub-criterion triggers documented discussion.
4. Moderation produces one agreed score per sub-criterion. Where agreement cannot be reached, the **lower** score applies and the disagreement is minuted.
5. Category scores are summed to a total out of 100. Cost (category H) is added after technical scoring is locked.

### 6.2 Thresholds

An option is recommendable only if **all** of the following hold:

| Test | Threshold | Rationale |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Mandatory gates | All 5 passed | Non-negotiable; MQ-3 may escalate rather than fail (§3.2) |
| Overall score | **≥ 60 / 100** | Below 60, the option requires more remediation than the status quo justifies |
| No category below 40% of its weight | e.g. D ≥ 6.8, C ≥ 5.6, A ≥ 6.4 | Prevents a strong aggregate masking a fatal weakness in one dimension |
| Category D (Privacy, Security & Compliance) | **≥ 60% of weight (10.2)** | Raised floor. Principles 7, 8 and 16 are rated CRITICAL and the residual compliance risks already exceed appetite |

**If no option meets the thresholds, Option D (retain both, bounded) is the recommendation**, and the RIFF review is asked to pause the request under [SGP-C1].

### 6.3 Cost Scoring (Category H, 15 points) — and Why It Cannot Complete Yet

| Sub-criterion | Pts | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **H.1 Five-year whole-of-life cost** | 8 | Licence + appliance + migration + support + exception, scored relative to the lowest compliant bid and to Option D as the do-nothing baseline |
| **H.2 Cost shape and predictability** | 4 | Capital versus recurring; per-room licensing versus appliance capex; renewal price protection offered (R-012) |
| **H.3 Transparency and lock-in** | 3 | Pricing model clarity; egress or export fees; uplift caps; cost of leaving |

> **Category H cannot be scored before the baselines land.** ARC-002-RSCH produced no 3-year TCO and named four blockers — D-1 (contract values), D-2 (Microsoft entitlement), D-3 (appliance inventory) and archive volume — the last of which lands **2026-08-28**. It further established that no specialist vendor publishes list pricing, so even with perfect internal baselines the cost columns require actual quotes obtained through Procurement.
>
> **The panel will therefore score categories A–F and I first, lock those scores, then open the cost envelope.** This is standard probity practice and here it is also a practical necessity. Any cost score produced before 2026-08-28 would be, in the words of the research, "an invented number wearing a table".

### 6.4 Tie-Break

Where two options are within 3 points of each other, the recommendation is decided in this order:

1. Higher score in category D (Privacy, Security & Compliance)
2. Higher score in category E (Portability, Migration & Exit) — the option that is easier to leave
3. Higher score in category C (Integration & Provisioning)
4. Lower whole-of-life cost

Ranking exit capability above cost is deliberate: Principle 9 holds that a platform that cannot be left cannot be rationalised, and this project exists because a previous generation of decisions did not consider exit.
