## 3. Context and Problem Statement

The engagement has produced a set of Must-priority capability gaps and a set of rationalisation candidates. What it has **not** produced is a rule for how a gap gets closed. Each gap can be closed by configuring capability the university already pays for, by extending an incumbent platform, by buying something new, or by building — and today that choice is made request by request, on functional merit, by whoever raised the request.

**Problem statement as a question**: *When a capability gap is identified, in what order must sourcing options be tested, and what evidence must be produced before a new recurring commitment is approved?*

### 3.1 Why this decision is needed now

Three pieces of evidence in the inputs converge on the same answer, and none of them is currently expressed as a rule:

1. **The estate already contains functionality that was paid for and never switched on.** `ARC-001-FINOPS-v1.0` §7.3 records this as lever L-2 and names Blackboard (6 categories), Articulate 360, MS Teams and Turnitin PeerMark as priority targets.
2. **Three prospective additions are queued, each carrying net-new recurring spend against a flat-envelope target** — Miro, OnExam and badging software, all currently at status "not in use, under investigation" [SL-C1]. Two of the three have plausible incumbent capability; one does not.
3. **Six platforms carry unresolved licensing or support-model questions** [SL-C2], including Articulate 360 whose enterprise licensing model is simply not understood. An estate cannot make a defensible buy decision while its own entitlement position is unknown.

Against that, REQ-035 (Should) requires total ecosystem licence spend to hold flat or reduce **while** Must-priority gaps close, and REQ-034 (Should) requires every vendor contract to permit export of all university data in open formats on termination. The second constrains the first: a platform that cannot be left cannot be rationalised, so its renewal is not a genuine decision and its acquisition is not a reversible one.

### 3.2 What this decision does *not* re-open

**ADR-001 already decided the integration mediation approach** — a central integration broker — and already defers the *purchase* of that broker to a **Principle 19 licensed-capability test** as Condition 1 of that decision. This ADR does not re-decide it, does not second-guess it, and does not relax it. What this ADR does is **generalise the same test from one decision into an estate-wide sourcing rule**, so that the next capability gap is not argued from first principles. ADR-001's Condition 1 becomes the worked example of Gate 1 in §5, and lever L-7 in `ARC-001-FINOPS-v1.0` §7.8 remains its owner.

### 3.3 An honest statement of how narrow "build" actually is

Most of this estate is commercial SaaS. Twenty-one platforms are in the estate today and `ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0` §C1.2 found all eight learning-capability categories sitting at Product or Commodity evolution, with mean differentiation pressure of 0.259 — meaning **no platform choice in those categories buys lasting advantage, and building one would be building a worse version of a commodity.**

The genuine build zone is small, and it is concentrated in the integration plane:

| Component | Market position | Sourcing |
|-----------|----------------|----------|
| Role authority service | Custom / Genesis — no market supplies it | **Build** (ADR-002) |
| Canonical data model | Custom / Genesis | **Build** |
| Capability and contract register, boundary register | Custom / Genesis | **Build** — a maintained spreadsheet with defined columns is sufficient |
| Integration glue between broker and platform APIs | Custom-built assembly of product APIs | **Build** |
| Integration broker itself | Product ~0.64, moving to ~0.76 | Buy or consume — subject to ADR-001 Condition 1 |
| The eight learning-capability categories | Product / Commodity | **Buy** |
| Discipline specialist tooling | Product, thin market | **Buy**; consolidation pressure does not apply |

Anyone reading this ADR expecting a build-versus-buy contest across the estate will be disappointed, and should be. **The real contest is not build versus buy. It is buy-again versus configure-what-we-already-bought** — and that is the contest this decision is designed to settle.

## 5. The Sourcing Hierarchy (the substance of the decision)

Five gates, tested **in order**. A gap may only be closed at gate *n* once gates 1 to *n−1* have been tested and the outcome recorded. "Tested" means evidenced against the capability map — not asserted by the requester.

| Gate | Test | Evidence required to pass through | Owner |
|------|------|-----------------------------------|-------|
| **1. Realise** | Is the capability already inside something we pay for, and can it actually be configured within the contract, support model and integration constraints? | Two-stage entitlement/configurability assessment. **Stage 2 is the one that gets skipped**; recording entitlement without verifying configurability overstates the lever | Dr. Benny Moog |
| **2. Extend** | Can an incumbent platform provide it with configuration, an in-term vendor roadmap item, or a modest paid module cheaper than a new platform on a whole-of-life basis? | Whole-of-life comparison including change, support and integration effort — not licence price alone | Dr. Benny Moog + Grace Tanaka |
| **3. Aggregate** | Is there a sector purchasing arrangement, or a shared-problem partner, before we go to open market alone? | A recorded answer from CAUDIT or equivalent, **including "no"**. Currently **never asked** | Grace Tanaka |
| **4. Buy** | Competitive process under University procurement policy, with the six standing contract requirements and the four conditions of adoption | Verified export, termination assistance, AU residency or written processing-location statement, SSO+MFA, published versioned interfaces, price-escalation cap. Plus WCAG 2.2 AA and APP 8 position | Grace Tanaka |
| **5. Build** | Only where no market supplies the capability, or where the capability is the university's own governance artefact | Named owner, documented and version-controlled, executable by at least two people (Principle 13) | Sam Okafor / Dr. Benny Moog |

**Gate 3 is the gate no one has used.** `ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0` §C1.3 records that no Australian sector purchasing arrangement was investigated during this engagement, and that every Australian university with a student information system, a timetabling system and an LMS faces a near-identical integration problem. Making it a gate rather than an idea is what converts it from an observation into an action.

### 5.1 The default posture, stated plainly

> **Exhaust already-licensed capability before acquiring anything new.**

Not "prefer buying over building" — that framing imports a UK service-standard default that does not describe this estate. Not "build to avoid licence cost" — that would be building commodities. The posture is that **the cheapest capability in this estate is the capability already on the invoice and switched off**, and the burden of proof sits with the request to acquire, not with the incumbent to defend itself.

### 5.2 Applying the hierarchy to the three queued acquisitions

| Platform | Category | Gate that applies | Position |
|----------|----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Miro** | Active Learning, Collaboration | **Gate 1** — Padlet, MS Teams whiteboard and Blackboard collaboration all plausibly hold this | Test before approving. FR-013 |
| **OnExam** | Assessment | **Gate 1** — ExamSoft and Blackboard assessment. Complicated by the landscape recording it as *not in use* while also requiring investigation into its *extent of use*, which is the signature of a tool already in use somewhere unrecorded [SL-C2] | Test entitlement **and** establish whether it is already running. FR-017 |
| **Badging software** (Badgr / Credly / Milestone) | Assessment | **No incumbent — a genuine gap.** Passes Gate 1 honestly, and reaches Gate 4 | But FR-019 is rated **Could**. Against a flat envelope with Must gaps open, **defer unless offset by a retirement**, and record the deferral with rationale |

The badging row is the important one, because it demonstrates the hierarchy is not a refusal mechanism. Badging genuinely fails Gate 1 — no incumbent provides it. It is deferred not because the gate blocked it but because a Could-priority acquisition carrying new recurring cost loses to Must-priority gaps under REQ-035. That is a priority judgement, made explicitly, and it is reversible the moment an offsetting retirement lands.

## 6. Considered Options

Three options were assessed: the staged hierarchy above, capability-led open-market acquisition, and Do Nothing as baseline. Genuine "build the platform ourselves" is not carried as a standalone option — §3.3 explains why it would be building commodities, and the residual build zone is already inside Option A at Gate 5.

### Option A: Staged sourcing hierarchy, default "exhaust licensed capability first"

**Description**: The five gates in §5, binding on the RIFF gate and on every renewal decision. Acquisition is permitted; it is permitted *last*, and against evidence.

**Implementation approach**: RIFF will not sign off a request that has not recorded an outcome at each preceding gate. The entitlement-versus-configuration assessment becomes a standing artefact per platform, held on the same record as cost, hosting region, export status and SSO conformance.

**Wardley evolution stage**: Not applicable — this is a governance posture, not a component. It exists to route components to the correct stage-appropriate sourcing.

**Good**:

- ✅ **The only option that can satisfy REQ-035 as written.** Flat-or-reduced spend *while* closing gaps is arithmetically unreachable if every gap is closed by acquisition
- ✅ Converts Principle 19 from a statement into an enforceable gate with named evidence and named owners
- ✅ Makes cost *avoidance* visible as a distinct line from cost *reduction* — the two are worth the same against a flat envelope, and only one is visible in a savings-only narrative
- ✅ Forces the six unresolved licensing questions to be answered, because Gate 1 cannot be assessed without them
- ✅ REQ-034 export verification becomes an acquisition precondition, so lock-in is priced before signature rather than discovered at exit
- ✅ Asks the CAUDIT question at Gate 3, which no one has yet asked
- ✅ Directly consistent with ADR-001 Condition 1 rather than parallel to it

**Bad**:

- ❌ **Slower to close a gap.** Gate 1 and Gate 2 take real time, and academics with an urgent teaching need will experience the hierarchy as an obstacle
- ❌ **Gate 1 cannot be assessed properly today.** Six licensing questions are open and no entitlement register exists; the gate is currently a promissory note
- ❌ Risks being read as a procurement freeze, feeding risk R-002 ("strategy perceived as cost-cutting", inherent 16)
- ❌ Configuring an incumbent to close a gap can produce a *worse* pedagogical outcome than a purpose-built tool, and the hierarchy does not by itself detect that — academic endorsement at Gate 1 is the only guard
- ❌ Creates real internal effort: entitlement assessment, configurability testing and export testing are all work that does not exist as funded activity today

**Cost analysis** *(indicative — no licence baseline exists; every figure below is a direction, not a magnitude)*:

| | Position |
|---|---|
| CAPEX | Internal effort: entitlement/configurability assessment, export testing, register construction. Overlaps the capability-map and register line already in `ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0` §D1.1 |
| OPEX | No new licence cost attributable to the posture itself. Configuration and support effort where Gate 1 or 2 succeeds |
| 3-year TCO | **Lowest of the three options**, and the only one with a mechanism for the licence envelope to fall. Avoids up to three new recurring commitments, and up to $150k per year on the broker line if ADR-001 Condition 1 succeeds |

### Option B: Capability-led open-market acquisition per gap

**Description**: Each Must-priority gap is closed by selecting the best available product for it, through a competitive process, on functional merit with cost as one weighted criterion. Incumbent capability is considered where someone raises it, but is not a gate.

**Implementation approach**: A procurement round per gap, RIFF assessing fit and cost at the point of selection.

**Wardley evolution stage**: Product (0.60–0.75) for the platforms acquired — stage-appropriate, which is genuinely a point in this option's favour.

**Good**:

- ✅ **Fastest closure of a genuine gap**, and best functional fit per gap taken individually
- ✅ Stage-appropriate: these categories are Product/Commodity and buying is the correct instinct for them
- ✅ Competitive process is defensible, preserves buyer power and is well understood by Procurement
- ✅ Avoids the failure mode where a configured incumbent half-meets a requirement and no one records that it half-meets it
- ✅ Lower internal effort than Option A — the vendor does the work

**Bad**:

- ❌ **Fails REQ-035 by construction.** Closing gaps by acquisition raises the envelope; there is no offsetting mechanism in this option
- ❌ **Buys capability the university already owns.** This is not hypothetical — it is the documented current state, with functionality paid for and never configured
- ❌ Deepens BR-001: adds platforms into categories that have no designated primary, with mean 4.6 core platforms per category already
- ❌ Each acquisition adds integration surface, credential surface, patch surface and an APP 8 assessment
- ❌ Weakest position on REQ-034 — acquisition momentum is exactly when export verification gets deferred to "post-signature"
- ❌ **Contradicts ADR-001 Condition 1**, which would have to be waived to adopt this posture consistently
- ❌ Directly contrary to Principle 19 and to the RIFF duplication rule [SGP-C1]

**Cost analysis**:

| | Position |
|---|---|
| CAPEX | Procurement effort per gap; implementation and integration per platform |
| OPEX | **New recurring licence cost per acquisition**, plus support and integration overhead. Up to three new commitments from the queued set alone |
| 3-year TCO | **Highest of the three.** Also the highest exit cost, since each addition is a new contract to leave |

### Option C: Do Nothing (baseline)

**Description**: No declared sourcing posture. Each request decided case by case on its own merits by whoever raised it, as today. Principle 19 remains a principle without a gate.

**Good**:

- ✅ No process change, no new internal effort, no perception of a procurement freeze
- ✅ Individual requests move quickly and requesters retain autonomy
- ✅ Genuinely retained as a baseline, not a strawman: the RIFF process already contains a duplication rule [SGP-C1], so the university is not starting from nothing

**Bad**:

- ❌ **This is the process that produced the current estate.** Twenty-one platforms, 0 of 8 categories with a designated primary, mean 4.6 core platforms per category, and functionality paid for and never switched on — every one of those was a reasonable individual decision
- ❌ Fails BR-007: RIFF continues to operate on opinion rather than on evidence against the capability map
- ❌ Leaves Principle 19 unenforceable, and leaves the RIFF duplication rule as a question that can be answered by assertion
- ❌ REQ-035 becomes unachievable and, worse, untestable
- ❌ The three queued acquisitions proceed on enthusiasm, and REQ-034 stays unverified for four platforms
- ❌ Leaves R-013 (duplication persists), R-016 (licensed capability not configurable) and R-029 (lock-in) unmitigated

## 7. Decision Outcome

**Chosen option: Option A — staged sourcing hierarchy with "exhaust already-licensed capability before acquiring anything new" as the default posture.**

### 7.1 Y-Statement

> In the context of **closing Must-priority capability gaps across a 21-platform commercial SaaS learning estate while total licence spend holds flat or reduces**,
> facing **an estate that already pays for functionality never switched on, three queued acquisitions carrying net-new recurring spend, and six unresolved licensing positions**,
> we decided for **a five-gate sourcing hierarchy that tests realise, extend and aggregate before buy, and reserves build for capability no market supplies**,
> and against **capability-led open-market acquisition per gap, and against leaving each request to be decided on its own merits as today**,
> to achieve **an enforceable Principle 19, a testable REQ-035, and exit rights priced before signature rather than discovered at termination**,
> accepting **slower gap closure, real unfunded internal assessment effort, a Gate 1 that cannot be properly assessed until the entitlement position is known, and a genuine risk of being read as a procurement freeze**.

### 7.2 Justification

**Why not Option B**, despite it being stage-appropriate and faster: it fails the one financial test the September business case has to pass. REQ-035 asks for flat-or-reduced spend *while* Must gaps close, and Option B has no mechanism by which the envelope can fall. It would also require ADR-001 Condition 1 to be waived, which would mean overturning a live decision in order to adopt a posture whose central weakness that condition exists to prevent.

**Why not Option C**, despite it being the least disruptive: it is the mechanism that produced the finding. Every platform in the current estate arrived through a defensible individual decision. The pattern is not a failure of judgement at any single point — it is the absence of a rule, and adding a rule is the only intervention that addresses it.

**Why Option A despite its cost in time and effort**: it is the only option under which "flat or reduced spend while gaps close" is arithmetically possible, and the only one that converts REQ-034 from a contract clause into a verified capability. The internal effort it requires is not new work invented by this ADR — the entitlement assessment, the register and the export testing are all already committed activity in `ARC-001-FINOPS-v1.0` §7 and `ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0` §D1.1. This decision makes them load-bearing rather than optional.

### 7.3 Risk appetite

`ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0` rejected full single-platform consolidation on **capability loss, not on cost**. This ADR sits in the same posture: it constrains *spend*, not *capability*. Where the two genuinely conflict, capability wins and the cost is recorded — which is why the badging deferral is written as reversible rather than as a refusal.

### 7.4 Explicit exclusions — what this posture does not touch

Stated as a boundary, because a cost-led sourcing rule will otherwise be applied where it does not belong:

- **Discipline specialist tooling is out of scope for consolidation pressure**: MuseScore, Ableton Live, iSimulate and Kuracloud. REQ-005, REQ-006 and REQ-010 describe capability general-purpose platforms do not provide, and `ARC-001-FINOPS-v1.0` §7.2 already excludes them from lever L-1. They remain subject to Gate 4's conditions of adoption — SSO, residency, export, accessibility — because Principle 4 is explicit that specialist need justifies a different tool, never a different architecture.
- **The integration mediation decision is not re-opened.** ADR-001 stands, including its Condition 1.
- **The Teams / Zoom / Echo360 rationalisation is a retirement decision, not a sourcing decision.** It belongs to BR-001 and lever L-1, and is not settled here.

### 7.5 Dissent anticipated

None recorded at time of writing. Two objections are expected and both are legitimate:

- **From academics**: that the hierarchy delays capability students need this teaching period. The answer is the RIFF exception path with a defined expiry, not an unrecorded workaround. An urgent need met by shadow acquisition is the worse outcome for everyone including the requester.
- **From Procurement**: that Gate 3 asks for a CAUDIT answer that may take longer to obtain than the gap can wait. The answer is that a recorded "no" satisfies Gate 3 — the gate requires the question to be asked, not a favourable outcome.

## 9. Validation and Compliance

### 9.1 How implementation will be verified

- **RIFF record audit**: every request reaching Gate 4 carries a recorded, evidenced outcome at Gates 1–3. A request without them is non-conformant regardless of merit
- **Renewal gate**: the §8.2 renewal decision gate in `ARC-001-FINOPS-v1.0` already blocks renewal without boundary status, hosting region and SSO conformance. This ADR adds that a renewal proposing *expansion* of scope is treated as an acquisition and enters at Gate 1
- **Conformance check**: `/arckit:conformance` after the first two gate-tested requests, verifying the recorded evidence exists and is specific
