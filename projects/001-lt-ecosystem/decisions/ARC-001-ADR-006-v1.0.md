## 1. Decision Title

**Cloud Platform Selection for University-Controlled Integration and Hosting Workloads**

Which cloud provider hosts the small set of components the University of Funk will itself run — the integration broker, the canonical schema registry, the reconciliation service, and the telemetry and log store — and what residency, security-maturity and exit position the university inherits as a result.

### 1.1 Relationship to ADR-005 — read these two together

ADR-005 (Deployment Topology and Environment Strategy for the Integration Plane) chose a **single Australian region, multi-availability-zone, with cross-region recovery from code**, on a three-tier environment model. It explicitly stated that "provider selection follows ADR-001 Condition 1 and is not pre-empted here", and recorded as its Assumption A-2 that **zone count must be verified against the chosen provider at selection, not assumed**.

**This ADR selects the provider and discharges that verification.** ADR-005 decides the shape; ADR-006 decides whose infrastructure that shape is built on. Neither is complete without the other, and the zone-availability caveat ADR-005 flagged turns out to be decision-relevant rather than cosmetic (§5, §6.3).

### 1.2 Scope — stated precisely, because the scope is the honest part of this decision

The L&T estate is overwhelmingly vendor-hosted SaaS. Blackboard, Echo360, Turnitin, ExamSoft, PebblePad, Zoom, Qualtrics, Leganto, Evasys, Sonia and the rest run where their vendors run them [SL-C1]. **This decision does not move any of them, and cannot.** Their hosting region is a procurement and contract attribute governed by DR-005 (residency register) and NFR-C-002 (APP 8 cross-border assessment) — not by a cloud platform choice.

**In scope** — the university-controlled workloads created by decisions already taken in this project:

| Workload | Origin | Character |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| Integration broker / event mediation | ADR-001 Option B | Persistent service; teaching-critical once INT-001 cuts over |
| Canonical schema registry | ADR-001, DR-001 | Small, stateful, low volume, high consequence |
| State reconciliation service | ADR-003 Option B layer 3 | Custom-built; queries authoritative source and derived copies |
| Telemetry, metrics and log store | ADR-003 Option B layers 1–2 | Volume-driven cost; holds derived personal information |
| Integration runtime / connector compute | ADR-001 phasing | Event-triggered; bursty around teaching-period boundaries |
| Non-production and sandpit substrate | ADR-005 three-tier model, INT-008 | Staff identity only; no student personal information |

**Out of scope**: re-platforming any vendor SaaS; the institutional data platform (INT-009 counterparty, owned outside L&T); the student information system (PeopleSoft) and timetabling (Allocate+); teaching-lab desktop fleets and lecture-theatre capture appliances (TC-4).

**The honest characterisation**: this is a *provider* decision covering roughly six components, not an enterprise cloud strategy. It matters disproportionately to its size because every one of those components sits on the path of personal information moving between systems, and because the broker becomes a shared teaching-critical dependency the moment INT-001 cuts over.

## 3. Context and Problem Statement

### 3.1 Problem description

ADR-001 chose a central integration broker. ADR-003 chose a three-layer observability plane with a custom reconciliation service. ADR-005 chose the topology those components deploy into. **None of them says whose cloud it is.**

That gap is not academic. The moment the broker holds a replay window of enrolment, role and grade events, and the moment the telemetry store retains derived engagement data, the university is holding personal information on infrastructure it has chosen — and APP 8 accountability, Essential Eight patching obligations, and the 99.9% teaching-period availability target all attach to that choice.

**Problem statement as a question**: Which cloud provider hosts the university-controlled integration and observability workloads, and does the chosen provider actually deliver the multi-availability-zone Australian topology that ADR-005 assumed?

### 3.2 Why this decision is needed now

1. **ADR-001 Phase 1 and ADR-005's build both stall without it.** Broker selection and standing up is a four-week phase; it cannot begin against an undecided provider. The 31 August 2026 roadmap (BR-003, BC-1) must record a hosting position or the September business case carries an unpriced line.
2. **ADR-005's Assumption A-2 is unresolved and load-bearing.** It requires zone count to be verified against the chosen provider. Until a provider is chosen, the topology ADR-005 selected is an aspiration. §5 discharges this for both active candidates, and the answer is not symmetric.
3. **Residency must be decided before data lands.** REQ-030 / NFR-C-001 prefers Australian residency, and Principle 8 requires cross-border disclosure to be "a decided position, not an inherited one". For vendor SaaS the university inherited its position; for workloads it controls, inheriting would be an unforced error.
4. **The default is worse than any option.** With no declared provider, each workload lands wherever its implementer or its vendor puts it — precisely the mechanism that produced twenty-plus tools across eight categories with no declared boundaries (BR-001, Principle 2).

### 3.3 What makes this decision unusual for this estate

The university has almost no experience of being the operator. Every current L&T platform is somebody else's problem to patch, back up and keep available. This decision creates the first L&T footprint where **the university is the operator** — a genuine cost, not a rhetorical one. Risk R-007 (single-person dependency on cloning automation) is evidence of how thinly this capability is currently held.

### 3.4 Regulatory and policy context

- **Privacy Act 1988, APP 8** — cross-border disclosure. Selecting an Australian region for university-controlled workloads *removes* an APP 8 trigger rather than requiring one to be assessed. Four data classes already trigger APP 8 through vendor SaaS [RQ-C10]; this decision must not add a fifth
- **Privacy Act 1988, APP 11** — security of personal information. The telemetry store and reconciliation service are new holdings under DR-003 and DR-006, subject to retention and minimisation (R-022)
- **ASD Essential Eight** — patch applications, patch operating systems, restrict administrative privileges, multi-factor authentication and regular backups all apply directly to university-operated cloud infrastructure. The estate sits largely at ML1 against an end-2027 ML2 target (R-020). Managed platform services shift the patching obligation to the provider; self-managed compute does not
- **RIFF governance** — the duplication rule applies: any new hosting commitment must justify why existing licensed or contracted capability is unsuitable [SGP-C1]. This is ADR-001 Condition 1 restated at the provider layer

### 3.5 Supporting links

- **Requirements**: BR-002, BR-004, BR-005, BR-007; NFR-A-001, NFR-A-002, NFR-P-001, NFR-S-001, NFR-SEC-001/002/003, NFR-C-001/002/003, NFR-M-001/002, NFR-I-001/002; DR-003, DR-004, DR-005, DR-006, DR-007; INT-001 to INT-009
- **Related ADRs**: ADR-001 (integration mediation — the workload this hosts), ADR-003 (observability — the second workload), ADR-005 (topology this provider must deliver), ADR-002 (authoritative role source), ADR-004 (third-party component policy)
- **Constraints**: TC-1 (vendor SaaS interface limits), TC-5 (integration delivery out of scope for this engagement), BC-2 (flat licence envelope), BC-4 (RIFF before commitment)

## 5. Considered Options

Three options are analysed: two active alternatives and a Do Nothing baseline. All candidate regions are Australian, per ADR-005 and DR-005.

1. **Option A** — Microsoft Azure: `australiaeast` primary, `australiasoutheast` recovery
2. **Option B** — Amazon Web Services: `ap-southeast-2` primary, `ap-southeast-4` recovery
3. **Option C** — Do Nothing (no declared provider; per-workload landing)

**Rejected at scoping, without full analysis** (recorded so the omission is visible, not silent):

- **Multi-cloud / best-fit-per-workload** — rejected because it multiplies the operating surface across a team that currently holds a single-person dependency on a course-cloning script (R-007). For six components, the optionality is not worth a second identity model, network path, cost model and on-call runbook. Supersede this ADR if a genuinely separable workload class emerges.
- **On-premises / campus data centre** — rejected because it fails NFR-A-001 without substantial capital (R-014 is already a live funding risk), and concentrates Essential Eight obligations the university is already failing at ML1 (R-020). Residency would be satisfied; nothing else would. ADR-005's topology assumes cloud regions in any case.
- **Google Cloud (`australia-southeast1` / `australia-southeast2`)** — technically capable and Australian-resident, and named in ADR-005's candidate set. Excluded at scoping on operating-model grounds: no enterprise relationship, no in-house skills, no procurement path. It fails Principle 19 more sharply than Option B without an offsetting advantage. Recorded as excluded, not as unsuitable.

### Option A: Microsoft Azure — `australiaeast` primary, `australiasoutheast` recovery

**Description**: The in-scope workloads run on Azure managed services in Australia East (Sydney), with Australia Southeast (Melbourne) as ADR-005's recovery region holding code, configuration and telemetry only.

**Implementation approach**: A landing zone with institutional single sign-on federated for platform administration (NFR-SEC-001, no local accounts), infrastructure defined as code in version control (Principle 13, NFR-M-002, ADR-005 recovery-from-code), managed messaging and event services for the ADR-001 broker, and a managed log and metrics store for ADR-003 layers 1–2. Reconciliation runs as event-triggered managed compute. Three-tier environments per ADR-005, with personal information prohibited outside production.

**Wardley evolution stage**: Commodity (0.80) — utility infrastructure, undifferentiated, priced by consumption.

#### Good (Pros)

- ✅ **Strongest Principle 19 position of the two active options.** The university holds a Microsoft agreement, and ADR-001 Condition 1 exists specifically to test whether it already provides integration or event-brokering capability. If it does, this consumes existing entitlement rather than creating new spend — the shortest route through BR-002 and R-013
- ✅ **Australia East supports availability zones** [AZ-C1], satisfying ADR-005's multi-AZ primary-region requirement and supplying the mechanism NFR-A-001's 99.9% target depends on, without leaving Australian jurisdiction
- ✅ **Identity continuity.** The institution's SSO and MFA position (NFR-SEC-001) extends to platform administration without a second identity model. R-019 (local accounts) is not re-created in new infrastructure, and NFR-SEC-003's single-view access review stays achievable
- ✅ **Coherent with the MS Teams rationalisation investigation** noted for 2027 [SL-C2] — not a reason to choose it, but it avoids a second strategic direction running against a live consolidation question
- ✅ **Managed services carry the patching obligation**, materially supporting the ML2 pathway (NFR-SEC-002, R-020)
- ✅ **Skills adjacency.** Digital & IT already operates a Microsoft-centric estate; the SD-7 sustainability test is easier to pass

#### Bad (Cons)

- ❌ **Concentration risk.** The university already depends heavily on Microsoft for productivity, identity and collaboration. Adding integration infrastructure deepens a dependency Principle 9 exists to keep bounded. R-029 (lock-in) applies at the platform layer, not only to SaaS
- ❌ **`australiasoutheast` does not offer availability zones** [AZ-C1]. This is the asymmetry ADR-005's Assumption A-2 anticipated. It is tolerable *only because* ADR-005 already designed the recovery region to hold code, configuration and telemetry with no running compute and no personal information at rest — under an active-active assumption it would be disqualifying
- ❌ **Consumption cost is harder to forecast** than a licence line, which is exactly the number the CFO scrutinises in the September business case (BR-002, SD-3)
- ❌ **The existing agreement may not cover it.** Condition 1 has not been run. If the agreement does not extend to the required services, this option's principal advantage evaporates and it becomes cost-comparable to Option B
- ❌ **Proprietary service surfaces invite gradual coupling.** Managed services are right for Essential Eight and wrong for exit unless open protocols are enforced from day one

#### Cost analysis *(indicative — no costing baseline exists for project 001; see §13 and ADR-001 A-1)*

| | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **CAPEX** | Landing zone design and build; identity federation; infrastructure-as-code baseline; skills uplift for two operators |
| **OPEX** | Consumption-based compute, messaging and log storage. Log retention is the dominant and most controllable variable — DR-006 retention rules directly reduce this line |
| **3-year TCO** | Lowest of the active options **if** Condition 1 confirms existing entitlement. Comparable to Option B if it does not |

### Option B: Amazon Web Services — `ap-southeast-2` primary, `ap-southeast-4` recovery

**Description**: The same workload set runs on AWS managed services in Sydney (`ap-southeast-2`), with Melbourne (`ap-southeast-4`) as ADR-005's recovery region. Both regions are Australian-resident.

**Implementation approach**: Equivalent landing zone with SSO federation for console and API access, infrastructure as code, managed event routing and message brokering for ADR-001 mediation, and managed log and metrics services for ADR-003. Verified: managed event routing, managed message brokering and event-triggered compute are all present in **both** `ap-southeast-2` and `ap-southeast-4` [AWS-C1] — so ADR-005's two-region Australian posture is real on this provider, not aspirational.

**Wardley evolution stage**: Commodity (0.82) — the most evolved candidate on breadth of managed primitives.

#### Good (Pros)

- ✅ **Two full Australian regions with the required managed services verified present in both** [AWS-C1], giving a genuinely Australian two-region posture with no APP 8 exposure and more headroom than Option A if the recovery posture ever needs to become active
- ✅ **Deliberate diversification** away from single-vendor concentration. This is the strongest Principle 9 position of the active options and the honest counter-argument to Option A
- ✅ **Mature managed messaging and event primitives**, well matched to the publish/subscribe pattern that INT-001 to INT-009 all specify
- ✅ **Broad open-standard support** (AMQP, MQTT, OpenTelemetry ingestion) suits ADR-003's explicitly vendor-neutral instrumentation standard
- ✅ **Managed services carry the patching obligation** identically to Option A — the Essential Eight argument does not distinguish the two

#### Bad (Cons)

- ❌ **Fails the Principle 19 test as currently understood.** No existing AWS enterprise relationship is known to exist. Every dollar is new spend against BR-002 and R-013, and the RIFF duplication rule [SGP-C1] would require justifying why the incumbent Microsoft position is unsuitable — an argument the evidence does not currently support
- ❌ **A second identity model to federate and operate.** Institutional SSO can reach it, but it is additional configuration, additional access-review surface (NFR-SEC-003 requires cross-platform review from a single view), and additional drift risk
- ❌ **Wider skills gap.** ADR-001 already records a skills-uplift risk; Option B enlarges it. Against SD-7 this is the weakest of the active options
- ❌ **Procurement path not established** — contracting, billing and exit terms from a standing start against a fixed 31 August deliverable (BC-1, R-004)
- ❌ **The diversification benefit is largely theoretical at this footprint.** Six components do not constitute a hedge; they constitute a second thing to operate

#### Cost analysis *(indicative)*

| | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **CAPEX** | Landing zone build, identity federation, procurement and contracting effort, wider skills uplift |
| **OPEX** | Consumption-based, broadly comparable to Option A at this volume; no offsetting entitlement |
| **3-year TCO** | Higher than Option A where Condition 1 confirms entitlement; comparable where it does not, plus a persistent second-platform operating overhead |

### Option C: Do Nothing (Baseline)

**Description**: No declared provider. Each workload lands wherever its implementer or its vendor puts it — the broker wherever the selected broker product is offered, telemetry wherever the monitoring tool happens to store it, reconciliation on whatever compute is at hand.

#### Good

- ✅ **No decision cost, no procurement, no landing-zone build.** Fastest possible start on any single workload
- ✅ **Maximum tactical flexibility** — each workload takes the path of least resistance at the moment it is built
- ✅ **No new platform for the team to learn**, at least initially

#### Bad

- ❌ **Fails Principle 8 (CRITICAL) directly.** Residency becomes inherited rather than decided, on workloads where the university has a completely free choice. It is precisely the failure Principle 8 was written to prevent
- ❌ **Fails DR-005.** Hosting region cannot be a recorded attribute of a platform nobody chose
- ❌ **Renders ADR-005 undeliverable.** A topology decision with no provider behind it cannot be built, and its Assumption A-2 can never be discharged
- ❌ **Risks adding a fifth APP 8 trigger.** A SaaS monitoring tool defaulting to a US region would put derived engagement data offshore without assessment, breaching NFR-C-002 and worsening R-022
- ❌ **Reproduces the estate's founding failure mode.** Twenty-plus tools with undeclared boundaries arose exactly this way — each choice locally reasonable, none made against a stated position (BR-001, Principle 2)
- ❌ **Availability becomes unmanageable.** NFR-A-001 cannot be asserted for a broker whose hosting resilience nobody specified. R-024 (outage during assessment) stays fully live
- ❌ **Essential Eight posture is unassessable** across scattered infrastructure, worsening R-020 against an end-2027 ML2 target
- ❌ **Exit is unplanned by construction**, engaging R-029 at the platform layer with no contractual counter

**Retained as a genuine baseline, not a strawman.** The RIFF pause provision permits closing a request, and deferring until ADR-001 Condition 1 completes is a defensible *tactical* position. But deferral is not the same as never deciding, and Do Nothing as a *destination* fails one CRITICAL principle, three Must requirements and a decision already taken.

## 6. Decision Outcome

### 6.1 Chosen option

**Option A — Microsoft Azure, `australiaeast` primary with `australiasoutheast` as the ADR-005 recovery region**, adopted as the declared cloud platform for university-controlled integration and observability workloads, **conditional on the Principle 19 test already required by ADR-001 Condition 1**.

The decision is **Proposed**, not Accepted. It carries five conditions (§6.4).

**What is being decided**: which provider hosts six university-controlled components, and that they run in Australia.
**What is not being decided**: anything about where vendor SaaS runs. That remains a procurement and contract matter under DR-005 and NFR-C-002, and this ADR neither improves nor worsens it.

**ADR-005 Assumption A-2 is discharged**: Australia East supplies availability zones [AZ-C1]; Australia Southeast does not. ADR-005's topology therefore holds on this provider **because** its recovery region was designed to hold code, configuration and telemetry only — not because the pairing is symmetric. That dependency is now explicit rather than assumed (§6.4 Condition 2).

### 6.2 Y-Statement

> **In the context of** an overwhelmingly SaaS-based L&T estate in which ADR-001 and ADR-003 created the first six components the University of Funk will itself operate, and ADR-005 chose a single-region multi-AZ topology while deliberately leaving the provider open,
> **facing** a CRITICAL residency principle that demands a decided rather than inherited position, a 99.9% teaching-period availability target that now attaches to a shared broker dependency, and a flat licence envelope that makes every new recurring line contestable,
> **we decided for** Microsoft Azure with `australiaeast` as the multi-availability-zone primary and `australiasoutheast` as the code-and-configuration recovery region, using managed services throughout,
> **and against** AWS in `ap-southeast-2` / `ap-southeast-4`, and against leaving the provider undeclared,
> **to achieve** Australian residency for every university-held copy of personal information in the integration path, an in-region availability mechanism that satisfies ADR-005's topology without leaving the jurisdiction, a patching obligation carried largely by the provider in service of the Essential Eight ML2 target, and the shortest defensible route through the BR-002 spend constraint,
> **accepting** a deepened concentration on a single vendor that Principle 9 exists to keep bounded, a recovery region without availability zones that constrains the university to ADR-005's recovery-from-code posture rather than anything warmer, consumption cost that is harder to forecast than a licence, and an operating obligation for a team whose current depth is evidenced by a single-person dependency on a course-cloning script.

### 6.3 Justification

**Why not Option C, despite zero decision cost.** Do Nothing fails Principle 8 — rated CRITICAL — on the one part of the estate where the university has a completely free choice. Everywhere else, residency was inherited from vendors chosen years ago for functional reasons. Here it is entirely elective, and choosing not to choose would put derived personal information offshore by accident. It would also leave ADR-005 as a topology decision with nothing to build on.

**Why not Option B, despite the genuinely better Principle 9 position.** This is the closest call in the document and the argument deserves fair statement. AWS offers two complete Australian regions with the required managed services verified present in both [AWS-C1] — a marginally stronger position than Azure's, since it leaves room for a warmer recovery posture later. Diversifying away from a single vendor is a real architectural good that Principle 9 endorses.

It loses on three grounds:

1. **Principle 19 is not a tiebreaker here; it is the governing rule.** The RIFF duplication test [SGP-C1] requires justifying why an incumbent is unsuitable before acquiring new capability. The Microsoft position is incumbent. No evidence exists that it is unsuitable — the test has not even been run. Choosing AWS *before* running that test would invert the university's own governance process, and this ADR is meant to be an instance of BR-007, not an exception to it.
2. **Operating surface is a first-order constraint at this footprint.** Six components do not justify a second identity model, a second network path, a second cost model and a second on-call rotation. R-007 is direct evidence of how thinly operational capability is held, and SD-7 asks for an architecture the Integration Architect can sustain.
3. **The diversification benefit does not materialise at this scale.** Concentration risk is real, but it is not meaningfully reduced by placing six components elsewhere while identity, productivity, collaboration and possibly lecture capture all remain where they are. Splitting the smallest workload class is the appearance of a hedge rather than a hedge.

**Why Option A despite the concentration objection.** The objection is legitimate and is recorded as dissent (§6.5), not dismissed. It is accepted for three reasons: the Principle 19 obligation is binding and points here; Australia East's availability zones supply the mechanism ADR-005's topology and NFR-A-001 both require, inside Australian jurisdiction [AZ-C1]; and lock-in at this layer is mitigable by design — open protocols, portable schemas and infrastructure as code are cheap to require at the outset and expensive to retrofit. Condition 3 makes them binding.

**On the availability-zone asymmetry.** It would be dishonest to present Australia Southeast's lack of zones as immaterial. It is material, and it constrains the university to exactly the recovery posture ADR-005 chose — code, configuration and telemetry, recovered by redeployment. If a future review wants a warmer recovery position, Azure's Australian region pair does not offer it and Option B's does. That is recorded as a review trigger (§11), not buried.

**Risk appetite alignment.** The project's posture treats CRITICAL principle breaches as unacceptable and cost overruns as treatable. Option A takes the conservative position on residency and availability while accepting a concentration risk that is visible, bounded and reviewable at renewal — consistent with how R-029 is treated elsewhere in the register (tolerate for existing, require open formats at renewal).

### 6.4 Conditions attached to this decision

1. **Principle 19 test completed before any procurement commitment.** ADR-001 Condition 1, extended to the provider layer: Digital & IT and Procurement confirm in writing what the existing Microsoft agreement covers for the required services. **If it does not cover them, this decision returns to RIFF** — Option A's principal advantage over Option B is the entitlement, and without it the two are close enough that the concentration objection may prevail. Owner: Cassandra Rhodes with Grace Tanaka. *This condition can invalidate the decision, not merely delay it.*
2. **The ADR-005 recovery posture is binding, not advisory.** Because `australiasoutheast` has no availability zones [AZ-C1], the recovery region holds code, configuration and telemetry only — no running compute, no personal information at rest — exactly as ADR-005 specified. A recovery-time objective must be stated and formally accepted against NFR-A-001 before the first integration cuts over. This restates ADR-001 Condition 2 with a now-specific regional constraint. Owner: Sam Okafor.
3. **Exit position designed in, not retrofitted.** Open protocols (AMQP or equivalent for messaging, OpenTelemetry for instrumentation per ADR-003), portable schema definitions, and all infrastructure as version-controlled code. Principle 9 and NFR-I-002 apply to this platform exactly as they apply to Blackboard. Owner: Sam Okafor, verified by Grace Tanaka at contracting.
4. **Privacy position for the new holdings signed off before build.** The telemetry store and reconciliation service create new university-held copies of derived personal information (DR-003, DR-006). Classification, minimisation, retention and automated deletion require Eleanor Frame's sign-off — the same requirement ADR-003 places on the observability plane, and not satisfied by residency alone. Owner: Eleanor Frame.
5. **No local administrative accounts, from the first resource.** Platform administration via institutional SSO with MFA (NFR-SEC-001, Principle 16). R-019 records two existing platforms breaching this; new infrastructure must not become a third. Owner: Tobias Ohm.

### 6.5 Dissent recorded

**Concentration risk — recorded, not resolved.** A reasonable architect would choose Option B on Principle 9 grounds, and the choice should be re-opened if the Principle 19 test fails. The counter-argument accepted here is that vendor concentration is a *portfolio* property and is not improved by scattering the smallest component set in the portfolio.

**Recovery-posture ceiling.** Selecting Azure caps the achievable Australian recovery posture at ADR-005's recovery-from-code model for as long as Australia Southeast lacks zones. Anyone who believes the university will want warmer recovery within the roadmap horizon should argue for Option B now rather than later, because migrating the substrate after cutover is materially more expensive than choosing it.

**Cost forecastability — anticipated from Finance.** Consumption-based hosting is harder to defend in a business case than a fixed licence. The mitigation is that log and telemetry retention is the dominant variable and is directly controlled by the DR-006 retention rules the university must set anyway. This should be tested at RIFF with a modelled figure, not asserted.
