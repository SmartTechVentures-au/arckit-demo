## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies the stakeholders of the Learning & Teaching Baseline Strategy engagement, their underlying drivers, how those drivers convert into measurable goals, and the outcomes that will demonstrate satisfaction. It provides traceability from individual motivation to project success metric, and it surfaces the conflicts that must be resolved before the September business case.

### Key Findings

Sixteen named stakeholders were analysed from the engagement register [ST-C1], plus four external parties inferred from the engagement context. Alignment on the *problem* is unusually strong — nobody disputes that the ecosystem is fragmented, that integrations are fragile, or that licensed capability is going unused. Alignment on the *solution* is materially weaker, and the single most consequential fault line is already documented: the CIO favours consolidation onto a single vendor platform, while the Director of Learning Technologies and the Dean of Music & Performing Arts defend best-of-breed pedagogical tooling [ST-C2].

That tension is not resolvable by analysis alone. It is a decision, it belongs in the WP6 decisions register, and the engagement's credibility depends on presenting it as a governed choice with explicit criteria rather than allowing it to be settled by whoever holds the budget.

A second, quieter risk: the compliance stakeholders (Privacy, Cybersecurity) hold MEDIUM formal influence but effective veto power. An unresolved APP 8 cross-border finding or an unremediated local-account exception can stop a platform decision late, after the business case is written.

### Critical Success Factors

- **CSF-1** — Architecture principles are agreed and academically credible before future-state work proceeds. WP7 and WP8 are both gated on this [ST-C3].
- **CSF-2** — The consolidation-versus-best-of-breed decision is resolved through the RIFF process with published criteria, not deferred into the roadmap as ambiguity.
- **CSF-3** — Privacy and security findings are raised early enough to shape platform decisions rather than invalidate them.
- **CSF-4** — The academic survey is visibly traceable into the recommendations. 412 respondents [ST-C4] will judge this engagement by whether their input changed anything.
- **CSF-5** — The 31 August deliverable is structured to feed the September business case directly, in the Executive's expected format.

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong alignment on problem definition and on the rationalisation objective. Genuine, documented disagreement on platform strategy, with a secondary tension between cost reduction and the investment required to fix integration. Alignment is sufficient to proceed, but the platform decision must be actively governed rather than assumed to resolve itself.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: Prof. Otis Hammond (DVC Education) — A defensible L&T strategy

**Stakeholder**: Prof. Otis Hammond, Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Education); Executive Sponsor

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Needs a coherent, evidence-based Learning & Teaching technology strategy that survives Executive scrutiny and converts into a funded September business case.

**Context & Background**: Hammond owns the L&T strategy and chairs the steering committee [ST-C1]. The ecosystem grew organically over roughly a decade without a governing architecture. A strategy that cannot explain *why* each tool exists — or that reads as an IT rationalisation exercise rather than an educational one — will not carry the Education Committee or the Executive.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Architecture principles agreed early and grounded in pedagogy, not just technology [ST-C3]
- Clear traceability from the 412-response academic survey to the recommendations [ST-C4]
- Recommendations structured to drop into the business case format

**Blockers**:

- Unresolved platform decisions leaving the future state ambiguous
- The strategy being perceived by academics as cost-cutting dressed as architecture
- Late-surfacing privacy or security findings that reopen settled decisions

**Related Stakeholders**: Groove (approves the resulting case), Clavinet (academic gate), Rhodes (funds delivery)

### SD-2: Cassandra Rhodes (CIO) — Eliminate fragility and reach security target

**Stakeholder**: Cassandra "Cas" Rhodes, Chief Information Officer

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / RISK

**Driver Statement**: Wants the integration estate rebuilt on sustainable foundations and the L&T estate brought to Essential Eight ML2, while reducing the number of platforms Digital & IT must support.

**Context & Background**: Rhodes owns Digital & IT, funds integration uplift, and has set an ML2 target across the SaaS-heavy L&T estate by end 2027 [ST-C6]. The current estate carries nightly batch transfers, manual CSV loads, manual grade re-keying, and undocumented scripts with single-person dependencies [ST-C7]. Rhodes favours consolidation onto a single vendor platform — a position that is a genuine strategic view but also, candidly, reduces the support surface her teams carry.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Integration architecture with a canonical data model giving a defensible target state
- Rationalisation that genuinely reduces platform count
- Executive backing for retiring tools that academics still like

**Blockers**:

- Best-of-breed advocacy from Moog and Key preserving overlapping platforms
- Insufficient capital for integration uplift if the CFO reads rationalisation as savings-only
- Platforms that cannot support institutional SSO remaining in the estate

**Related Stakeholders**: Okafor (delivers it), Ohm (security posture), Moog and Key (opposed on consolidation), Ostinato (funds it)

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1 — Platform consolidation versus best-of-breed** (SD-2 versus SD-6, SD-13). Rhodes favours consolidating collaboration, delivery and capture onto a single vendor platform; Moog and Key defend specialist tools chosen for pedagogical capability [ST-C2]. These are incompatible for the overlapping categories: one platform cannot be both retired and retained.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Route to WP6 as a formal decision with criteria published *before* options are scored, weighting pedagogical capability, integration cost, security conformance, and whole-of-life licence cost. Complete capability mapping first (G-2) so the comparison rests on evidence rather than advocacy. Whichever way it resolves, the outcome is a *declared boundary* — the architecture principles require the decision to be explicit, which is itself a resolution of the current ambiguity. Escalate to Education Committee if RIFF cannot converge; do not carry it into the business case unresolved.

- **Conflict 2 — Cost reduction versus integration investment** (SD-3 versus SD-2, SD-7). Ostinato requires flat or reducing spend; fixing the integration estate requires capital investment that produces no licence saving.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Separate recurring licence spend from one-off integration investment in the business case, and fund integration uplift partly from rationalisation savings. Sequence quick wins — the manual placement grade flow and provisioning automation — early, so demonstrable operational benefit precedes the larger canonical-model investment.

- **Conflict 3 — Compliance requirements versus pedagogical tool retention** (SD-10, SD-11 versus SD-6, SD-13). Platforms permitting local accounts or disclosing personal information offshore may be exactly the tools academics value most.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Assess privacy and security conformance during capability mapping, not after platform decisions. Where a valued tool fails a criterion, present three options explicitly — remediate, accept risk with a dated review, or retire — and take the decision at RIFF with the affected school present. Avoid the failure mode where compliance objections arrive late and appear obstructive.

- **Conflict 4 — Delivery deadline versus consultative depth** (SD-9 versus SD-8, SD-15, SD-16). Requirements mapping is workshop-based and consultation takes time; 31 August is fixed.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Prioritise systems ruthlessly in week one, timebox workshops, and be explicit in the deliverable about which systems received depth analysis and which received desk review. Do not silently narrow scope — an honest coverage statement protects the engagement's credibility more than an implied completeness it did not achieve.

- **Conflict 5 — Consistency mandate versus academic autonomy** (SD-16, SD-4 versus SD-15). Template conformance serves students but reads to teaching staff as mandated rework.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Frame consistency as applying to the student-facing surface only, with pedagogical variation permitted and recorded. Make the compliant path the easiest path through templates and automated cloning rather than through audit. Involve Castle in the principles workshop so the requirement is shaped with academics rather than imposed on them.

**Synergies**:

- **Synergy 1**: Rhodes (SD-2) and Ostinato (SD-3) both benefit from rationalisation for different reasons — support burden and licence cost. Their alignment carries G-2 and G-7 even while they disagree about integration capital.
